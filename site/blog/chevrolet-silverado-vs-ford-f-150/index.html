Chevrolet Silverado vs Ford F-150: An Honest Comparison for BC Buyers

By Dan · Personal GM Consultant, Coquitlam BC · Updated 2026-10-06

This is the heavyweight fight of Canadian trucks, and you deserve better than brand loyalty disguised as advice. The F-150 is a brilliant truck that outsells everything; the Silverado is a brilliant truck that GM keeps making sharper. They are closer than the internet argues, and the real differences show up in specific places: price on comparable trims, cabin layout, and what your local dealer can actually do for you after the sale. Since you are reading this in the Lower Mainland, you will also want the BC-specific bits at the end.

Engines and capability

Both lineups cover the same ground: turbocharged base engines, V8 muscle, and off-road flagship trims (ZR2 for Chevrolet, Raptor for Ford). Real-world towing for equivalently equipped full-size trucks lands in the same neighbourhood, and payload differences are smaller than spec-sheet cherry-pickers suggest. The practical advice: choose the engine for your actual towing weight and annual kilometres, then compare prices on that specific configuration, because that is where the brands genuinely separate.

Where the Silverado pushes back

  • Price on comparably equipped trims frequently favours Chevrolet, especially mid and upper trims
  • Multi-Flex tailgate is the most useful bed innovation in the segment
  • GM's full-size cabin and controls layout rewards long-term owners
  • One dealership relationship for both GMC and Chevrolet under the same roof at Eagle Ridge GM

Where the F-150 earns its crown

  • Enormous selection: every configuration exists, used and new, everywhere
  • Pro Power Onboard generator is genuinely handy for work sites
  • Strong BC resale on popular trims
  • A loyal service network with deep F-150 familiarity

The BC buyer's tiebreakers

Here is what tips these decisions in Metro Vancouver in real life: first, financing fit, the truck whose payment fits your file wins, and trim-for-trim pricing often gives Silverado the edge. Second, service relationship: one GM dealership handles sales, financing programs, and service under one roof, and continuity shows up over an eight-year ownership. Third, honest advice: a consultant who sells both GMC and Chevrolet (and will tell you when a used F-150 trade already on the lot is the right answer) beats brand loyalty every time. Ask what just traded in; the best cross-brand bargains arrive quietly.

The verdict, in one line

Two great trucks: F-150 wins on selection and work-site tech, Silverado pushes back on trim-for-trim price and bed innovation. Your financing file picks the real winner.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Silverado cheaper than the F-150?

Frequently yes on comparably equipped mid and upper trims, both new and used. The exceptions are high-demand configurations; compare specific units rather than starting prices.

Which tows more, Silverado or F-150?

Maximum ratings are close and depend on configuration. Match the engine and towing package to your trailer's actual weight; both handle the loads most BC owners pull.

Is the F-150 more reliable than the Silverado?

Both are proven platforms with strong records when maintained. Reliability differences between comparable years are small; service history matters more than the badge.
