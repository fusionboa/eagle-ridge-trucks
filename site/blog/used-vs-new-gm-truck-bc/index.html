Used vs New GM Truck in BC: Which One Actually Makes Sense?

By Dan · Personal GM Consultant, Coquitlam BC · Updated 2026-10-06

Every truck buyer hits this fork, and most advice online is either 'always buy used' or 'new is safer', which is to say, no advice at all. The honest answer is that the right choice depends on three numbers: how many kilometres you drive, how long you keep vehicles, and what payment your financing file supports. In the Lower Mainland specifically, the used GM market has quirks that swing the math further toward used than in most of Canada. Here is the actual decision framework, not a slogan.

The depreciation math, plainly

A new truck takes its steepest value drop in years one and two; a two-year-old truck lets the first owner absorb that drop for you. If you keep vehicles for eight-plus years, the gap narrows and new-car warranty has real value. If you trade every three to four years, buying two-to-four-year-old used units repeatedly is usually the cheapest way to drive a late-model truck, because somebody else keeps paying the new-car penalty.

For buyers rebuilding credit or new to Canada, the used route is usually the faster path to an approval that fits, because lenders look at payment-to-income first. A lower principal can matter more than a slightly better rate, and that math favours used in most files Dan prices.

When new is genuinely worth it

  • You keep vehicles a decade: warranty coverage through the expensive years
  • You drive 30,000+ km a year and want the freshest start on the odometer
  • Specific tech or safety package matters to you (new bodies carry the newest cabins)
  • Incentive programs at the moment of purchase (ask Dan what is live this month)

The Lower Mainland used-market quirk

Because supply of clean, one-owner local trucks is thin relative to demand, good used GM units here move fast and hold value, which cuts two ways: your used purchase depreciates slower, but the good ones do not wait around. This is exactly why Dan's list gets first look at incoming trades before they are broadly advertised. If you are payment-driven, ask him to watch for the specific truck you want rather than settling for whatever is on the lot today.

The verdict, in one line

Keep it a decade or drive huge km: buy new. Trade often or building credit: buy 2-4 year old used. The Lower Mainland market rewards whoever watches incoming trades.

Frequently asked questions

Is a used truck a bad idea for financing?

No. Used vehicles are financed daily, and lower prices often make approvals easier. Rates can be slightly higher than new-car programs, but the smaller principal usually wins on total cost.

How old is too old for a financed used truck?

Lenders each have age and term limits, but plenty of programs cover late-model used trucks comfortably. Dan matches the unit to the program, not the other way around.

Do used GM trucks come with warranty?

Many late-model units carry remaining factory coverage, and extended options exist. Check the specific VIN; Dan pulls coverage before you commit.

Is the used market in BC still expensive?

Prices normalized from the peak, and the LTD/runout effect plus steady trade-ins keep fair options available. Clean one-owner local units still move fast.
